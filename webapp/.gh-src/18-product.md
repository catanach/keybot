Flagging the process gap first, plainly: this was never groomed. Nobody asked Rosy to approve Option A over B/C, and the issue is still open while three commits (dfa42e0, 7091437, 08f842b) are already on the board. That's a real problem as a pattern even though the outcome here looks fine — a firmware change that bricks the board is not something to find out about after the fact. Going forward, "device now owns its own filesystem" is exactly the kind of call that should stop and wait for Rosy, not just Product.

Given that it's done, I checked the acceptance criteria against the actual code rather than the comments' word for it:

- **UI text matches what's claimed.** `webapp/app/templates/index.html:170-179`: the sidebar has a collapsed "Firmware" section with a "Deploy to device" button (hint: "Deploy code to device. Lets current loop finish first.") and, below it, a button literally labeled "Hand the drive back to the Mac" (hint: "The Pico owns its own drive, which is what lets deploys work. Hand it back if you need to drag files onto CIRCUITPY yourself."). That's accurate to what `boot.py` and `main.py:600-619` actually do.
- **README matches the code.** The "Updating the firmware" section describes all three recovery paths, and it matches `src/boot.py`'s own comments word for word, including the corrected claim that a Pico W has no reset button. Good — the wrong "press reset twice" instruction from comment 1 did not make it into the shipped docs.
- **`deploy.sh` tells the truth now too.** It prints a note that CIRCUITPY is about to go read-only on the Mac and how to get it back, right after copying `boot.py` (`deploy.sh:66-74`). That satisfies the acceptance line about the deploy script not surprising anyone.

So the truth-telling bar is met. The one usability gap I'd want acknowledged, not necessarily blocking:

- **"Hand the drive back to the Mac" only helps if the board still answers.** If it doesn't, Rosy's only paths are "unplug and replug it TWICE in quick succession, second time within about a second of the first" or holding BOOTSEL (which wipes the board). Both are exact physical timing/handling on the hardware itself — which is the opposite of the goal stated in the original issue ("the whole point of this project is that Rosy should not have to go and physically handle the board"). That's an inherent cost of Option A, already named in the issue, not a new bug — but it means the acceptance bar of "a firmware deploy from the webapp works" is met, while the *original* goal ("Rosy never touches the board") quietly is not, for the failure case. Worth saying out loud rather than letting it pass silently.

Recommendation: close #18 with a comment noting the retroactive approval (Option A, accepted after the fact) and the one open cost above. It unblocks #16 and #17 as stated, and re-litigating the decision now that it's shipped, tested, and documented wouldn't change anything on the board. If the "no physical handling" goal still matters to Rosy, that's worth its own small issue — e.g., a way to trigger safe-mode remotely, or at least a webapp banner when `/status` stops answering that walks through the two remaining physical steps in plain language — rather than reopening this one.
